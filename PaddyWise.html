The idea

One platform for the whole season.

Farmers already have pieces of advice. What they lack is one place that connects soil, disease and market data into a single decision.

PaddyWise is a final-year research project at the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT). It brings four research components together: IoT field monitoring with fertilization guidance, smart crop recommendation, disease detection with severity estimation, and disease-aware harvest yield estimation.

Most Sri Lankan paddy decisions still rely on experience and fixed guidelines. That leads to fertilizer applied at the wrong time, diseases noticed too late, and crops chosen without knowing what they will sell for. Existing tools such as e-Krushi, GeoGoviya and Krushi Advisor offer static advice. None of them combines live field data, disease severity and market prices.

All four components share a central cloud database and REST APIs, so the output of one module becomes the input of the next. The farmer sees everything on one dashboard.

PaddyWise supports farmer decisions. It does not replace advice from Department of Agriculture officers.

Four components, one field

Meet the research components.

Each team member owns one component. Together they cover the season from the soil to the selling price.

Component 1

IoT Field Monitoring & Fertilization Guidance

ESP32 sensor devices placed at the start, middle and end of the field measure the soil continuously and tell the farmer when to fertilize and how much to use.

Monitors
Soil moisture, soil temperature, pH, electrical conductivity, NPK and humidity
Predicts
Fertilization timing (Random Forest) and fertilization quantity (XGBoost regression). Quantity model reached 90% accuracy at Progress Presentation 1.
Delivers
Dashboard alerts in Sinhala and English, with organic or bio-fertilizer alternatives when chemical supply is limited

Disease Detection & Severity Estimation

The farmer uploads a photo of a paddy leaf. The system names the disease and measures how far it has spread across the leaf.

How PaddyWise works

From a sensor reading to a decision.

Data moves through the four components in order, and actual harvest results feed back to improve the models.

  1. Sense

    Field devices stream soil and climate readings to the cloud.

  2. Recommend

    Soil, weather and prices rank the crops that suit the field.

  3. Detect

    A leaf photo returns the disease and its severity grade.

  4. Estimate

    Severity and field data predict yield under three scenarios.

  5. Decide

    The dashboard shows alerts, costs and the recommended action.

The research domain

Grounded in the field. Built on evidence.

The literature, gaps, questions and methods behind PaddyWise.

Research notebook

Decision support for Sri Lankan paddy cultivation.

The project follows Design Science Research: identify the problem, define objectives, design and build the system, demonstrate it in the field, evaluate it and communicate the results.

RESEARCH QUESTIONHow can an integrated decision support system using IoT sensors, machine learning and market data help Sri Lankan paddy farmers make real-time, economically sound decisions on crop choice, fertilization and disease treatment?
Literature survey

The review covered more than nine scholarly sources and the main Sri Lankan agriculture platforms across four areas: IoT field monitoring, crop recommendation, paddy disease detection and yield prediction.

  • IoT soil monitoring systems collect useful data but focus on irrigation, not fertilizer decisions.
  • Jayasinghe & Ranasinghe (2021) reached 89.3% accuracy on Sri Lankan field images, but only for healthy versus diseased.
  • Abdurrahman & Miyim (2025) and Ekanayake et al. (2021) validated yield prediction from weather data, without disease severity as an input.
  • The IRRI Standard Evaluation System provides severity-to-yield-loss tables, but not linked to live sensor data or local varieties.
  • e-Krushi, GeoGoviya and Krushi Advisor give static advice with no real-time data, severity grading or market forecasting.
Research gap

No single platform addresses all four dimensions of paddy farm decisions at once.

G1Fragmented solutions. No platform integrates IoT, climate, disease and market intelligence.
G2Raw IoT data. Sensor readings are shown without actionable, localised recommendations.
G3No severity or economic impact. Disease tools do not grade severity or forecast yield loss.
G4Market-blind crop choice. Recommendations ignore price changes and supply-demand.
Research problem

Sri Lankan paddy farmers face income instability and lower productivity because of unpredictable weather, soil imbalances and fungal diseases. They rely on traditional knowledge and fixed guidelines instead of real-time field data, which leads to poorly timed fertilizer and late disease detection.

Rice Blast, Brown Spot and uninformed crop selection can cause yield losses above 30% in severe seasons. Existing systems are fragmented: monitoring tools give no decision support, disease tools give no severity assessment and crop tools ignore market economics.

Research objectives

Main objective: Develop an integrated, context-aware, IoT-based decision support system that helps Sri Lankan paddy farmers make data-driven and economically optimised cultivation decisions.

  1. Monitor soil moisture, pH, NPK and temperature continuously and generate real-time fertilization alerts.
  2. Combine IoT data, climate forecasts and market price trends to recommend economically viable crops.
  3. Detect Rice Blast and Brown Spot and grade severity on a 0–4 scale with management recommendations.
  4. Predict yield under healthy, untreated and treated scenarios to support evidence-based treatment decisions.
Methodology & data

Design Science Research guides the work, combining iterative model development, experimental validation and system engineering.

  • IoT sensor data: soil moisture, pH, NPK, temperature and humidity
  • Weather APIs: forecasts, rainfall and temperature trends
  • Paddy leaf images collected locally and annotated with DoA-SL officers
  • Historical and forecast market price data
  • IRRI Standard Evaluation System disease–yield loss tables
  • Department of Agriculture guidelines and historical yield data

Ethics: farmer consent forms, data anonymisation and SLIIT ethics clearance. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.

Commercialization
  • Customers: smallholder paddy farmers aged 25–55, younger tech-adopting farmers and cooperatives in the main paddy districts.
  • Market: more than 1.8 million paddy farming households in Sri Lanka.
  • Revenue: freemium model with a free basic tier and premium at LKR 500–1,500 per month, plus cooperative and B2B subscriptions and API licensing for insurers and government.
